Monitoring Brain Architecture

The brain is the part of the system that understands the marketplace. It keeps a graph of metrics and how they depend on each other, computes any metric for any segment from the simulator's data, and will use both to notice a change, trace it to a cause and report it without being asked.

  • 36 metrics, 294 typed edges
  • 8 modules, 1395 lines
  • 102 tests
  • foundation built; monitor, investigator and extension agent planned

From data to incident

Two things meet in the middle: the model of the business on top and the data path below. Solid boxes exist and are tested; dashed boxes are the next milestones.

The metric graph

Drawn from the graph the code loads, not by hand. Every line downward is an exact identity, so a change at the top can be split into the parts each factor explains.

  • Formula edges carry a role: contribution = marketplace_fees + ad_spend − discounts, and everything below is a product. The six boxes in the bottom row are the step rates of the funnel; a fault in one step shows up in exactly one of them.
  • gmv and sessions feed two parents. The graph is a DAG, so the effect of a factor on the outcome is summed over all paths: gmv raises fees and raises discounts, with opposite signs at the top.
  • Possible causes are hypotheses with a sign: more stock-outs should lower the add-to-cart rate. They stay assumed until an incident tests them; a question mark means the direction is unknown.
  • Context links a metric to releases of the systems behind it, to the freshness of its data sources and to the promo calendar. A derived metric inherits the sources of its parts.

One tick of the planned loop

The order of the steps is the algorithm. Each step uses what the foundation already provides: the sources of a metric, sums that can be turned into any metric, and the typed edges.

What "validated" means

The graph is checked against the data every time it loads. The report separates two kinds of result, measured here on 28 days of simulator data.

52checks on dataThey can fail: a metric has values in at least 95 % of the hours, values stay within bounds, a contributed SQL agrees with its formula.
28hold by definitionNo data can break them: orders = sessions × (orders / sessions). They show the definitions are consistent and are not presented as evidence.
